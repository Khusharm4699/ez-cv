# Gap Coverage Plan (method + template)

When a JD must-have has no evidence in `profile.json`, it is a **gap**. Gaps are
flagged and closed honestly — never papered over with invented experience.

## How to build the plan

1. From `reference/jd-analysis-method.md`, list every must-have and nice-to-have.
2. For each, mark the status:
   - **Covered** — real evidence exists; point to the bullet/skill.
   - **Adjacent** — you have transferable evidence; reframe truthfully.
   - **Gap (easy)** — closable with a small project or focused study in days.
   - **Gap (hard)** — needs real time on the job; be honest in interviews.
3. Rank gaps by `severity = how central it is to the role`.
4. For each closable gap, attach a concrete action with a deadline.

## Template table

| # | Requirement | Status | Evidence / Plan | Severity |
|---|-------------|--------|-----------------|----------|
| 1 | <must-have> | Covered | <profile bullet> | — |
| 2 | <must-have> | Adjacent | <how to reframe truthfully> | Low |
| 3 | <must-have> | Gap (easy) | <small project to ship this week> | Medium |
| 4 | <must-have> | Gap (hard) | <honest framing; longer-term plan> | High |

## Closing plan (example shape)

- **This week.** Ship one small, public project that demonstrates the most
  central easy-gap skill. Add it to `projects` with a real link and metrics.
- **Next two weeks.** Do a focused study sprint on the next gap; capture a write-up
  or demo you can point to.
- **In interviews.** For hard gaps, name them plainly and show the adjacent
  evidence plus your learning plan. Honesty beats a resume that wins an interview
  it can't survive.

## Hard rule

A gap never becomes a fabricated bullet. It either gets closed with real work or
gets surfaced honestly.
